## Overview

As the aviation and defense sectors accelerate toward uncrewed aircraft systems (UAS), Advanced Air Mobility (AAM), and safety-critical autonomous flight, the aerospace industry faces an unprecedented shortage of engineers trained in Model-Based Systems Engineering (MBSE) and formal software/hardware verification. Developed by the ASSIST Laboratory at the Florida Institute of Technology (Florida Tech) under the NASA University Leadership Initiative (ULI) TRANSCEND project, SysMod Studio serves as a direct workforce development bridge. It transforms pre-college engineering experiences at FIRST Robotics Competitions (FRC) into a talent pipeline, equipping students with the exact MBSE workflows, formal verification methods and system architecture skills required for the future of autonomous aviation.

### Context & Motivation

The TRANSCEND (Trustworthy Resilient Autonomous ageNts for Safe City transportation in the Evolving New Decade) initiative is a multidisciplinary, multi-institution effort focused on bridging the gap between the Current State of FAA Aircraft Certification Regulations and AAM Industry Needs and the Future State: AAM Certification Processes for LearningEnabled Increasingly-Autonomous Systems. The project supports In-time System-wide Safety Assurance (ISSA) across three foundational assurance goals:

1. Trustworthy: Ensuring system outputs (control loops, power routing, actuator signals) are accurate, robust, and mathematically reliable under edge conditions.

2. Believed: Ensuring operators, safety inspectors, and regulatory authorities appropriately believe what the system is indicating through transparent trade-off matrices and explicit modeling.

3. Relied Upon: Ensuring generated system outputs, bus topologies (CAN, CAN FD), and hardware architectures are useful, actionable, timely, and fully compliant with safety envelope limits.

SysMod Studio operationalizes TRANSCEND’s core pillars—Human-Computer Interaction, Explainable AI, ML-based Analyses, and Formal Methods—to build a trained workforce capable of creating scientific knowledge and safety/integrity analysis tools for safety-critical autonomous systems.

### Problem Statement
Traditional MBSE tools (e.g., Enterprise Architect, Cameo Systems Modeler) carry steep learning curves, expensive licensing, and disconnected code execution layers that make them inaccessible to high school students. Consequently, early-stage robotics programs rely on ad-hoc trial and error, missing out on formal systems engineering training. SysMod Studio closes this gap by embedding professional aerospace MBSE practices into an accessible workspace tailored to FRC teams.

### Solution Statement
SysMod Studio connects high school FIRST Robotics strategy with aerospace-grade engineering rigor. Through a four-phase visual workflow, FRC students define physical bounds, execute trade studies, build graphical node-and-edge electrical/control architectures, run real-time rule checks, SysMod Studio White Paper ASSIST Laboratory and export production code (WPILib Java, Python) alongside formal Architecture Analysis & Design Language (AADL) models used in aviation safety analysis.
